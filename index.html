Mechanical Ventilator Cheatsheet
Concept—Basics
  • Core idea: oxygenation ← FiO₂ + PEEP; ventilation ← tidal volume + rate.
  • Modes:
    What's controlled
    VCtidal volume fixed — pressure varies with compliance/resistance.
    PCinspiratory pressure fixed — tidal volume varies.
    PRVCauto-adjusts pressure breath-to-breath to hit a set VT.
    How breaths are delivered
    ACevery breath, patient- or machine-triggered, gets a full set breath.
    SIMVmandatory breaths + patient's own spontaneous breaths mixed in.
    CPAP/PSVspontaneous only, pressure-support assisted, no mandatory rate.
  • Compliance: how easily the lung expands — low compliance (stiff lung) needs more pressure for the same VT.
  • Pressures: Peak = resistance + compliance; Plateau (post-pause) = compliance/alveolar pressure. High peak + normal plateau → resistance; high peak + high plateau → stiff lung.
  • Minute ventilation: MV = VT × RR — the number that clears CO₂.
  • Inspiratory flow: higher flow → shorter inspiration → more time to exhale; helpful in COPD. I:E ≈ 1:2 normal, longer E-time if obstructive.
  • Waveforms: on flow-time, expiratory flow not returning to zero before the next breath = air trapping/auto-PEEP.
  • Hemodynamics: positive pressure ventilation can drop venous return and BP, especially with high PEEP or low volume status.
Strategy—Lung Protective Ventilation

Low tidal volume + adequate PEEP + limited plateau pressure protects the lung.

  • VT target: 6 mL/kg PBW (acceptable range 4–8 mL/kg) — use predicted, not actual, body weight.
  • Plateau pressure: keep below 30 cmH2O.
  • Driving pressure (Plateau − PEEP): keep below 15 cmH2O.
  • PBW:Male50 + 2.3×(height in − 60) Female45.5 + 2.3×(height in − 60)
Strategy—ARDS & COPD
ARDS — small, stiff lung
  • Low VT (6 mL/kg PBW), adequate PEEP, avoid overdistension, plateau < 30 cmH2O.
COPD / Asthma — air trapping
  • Give more time to exhale: lower rate, higher inspiratory flow; watch for auto-PEEP; PEEP set carefully.
  • Waveform clue: expiratory flow not returning to zero before next breath.
Algorithm—CO₂ & Acidosis Workup

High CO₂ / low pH not improving → ABG → vent delivery → mechanics → physiology, in that order.

  • 1. ABG: confirm trend; acute vs chronic; look for a mixed picture.
  • 2. Delivery: check RR, VT, minute ventilation, mode, disconnect/circuit.
  • 3. Mechanics: peak vs plateau + waveform — resistance vs compliance vs air trapping.
  • 4. Physiology: air trapping, dead space, sepsis/fever, shock, metabolic load.
  • Winter's formula: expected PaCO₂ ≈ 1.5×HCO3 + 8 (±2).
  • Acute resp. acidosis: HCO3 rises ~1 per 10 mmHg ΔPaCO₂ • chronic: ~3.5 per 10.

📊 Dengue Burden in Bangladesh

2023 — Largest Ever Outbreak

  • 321,179 confirmed cases; 1,705 deaths
  • CFR 0.53% — among highest globally
  • 74% deaths due to DSS; 81% within 3 days of admission
  • Peak Sep 2023: 79,598 cases, 396 deaths

2024 Update

  • 101,214 admitted; 575 deaths
  • 19.3% cases in children (0–15 yrs)
  • Den-2 dominant (70%), Den-3 (20%), Den-4 (9%)
  • Now year-round nationwide — no longer seasonal
Up to 80–90% of dengue infections are asymptomatic. Only <5% progress to severe forms — these unreported cases drive silent community transmission.
🦠 Pathophysiology — Key Mechanisms

Severe disease — especially in secondary heterotypic infection — is driven by:

  • ADE (antibody-dependent enhancement) — non-neutralising antibodies ↑ viral biomass
  • Cytokine storm — CD4+/CD8+ T cells → IFN-γ, TNF-α, lymphotoxin
  • Complement activation — DENV NS1 disrupts endothelium via TLR4
  • Vasculopathy → capillary permeability → plasma leakage → haemoconcentration, effusions, DSS
  • Coagulopathy → ↑aPTT, ↓fibrinogen, DIC, thrombocytopenia
  • Cytopathy → leucopenia, thrombocytopenia; Organopathy → MOF
Secondary infection with DENV-2 or multiple serotypes significantly increases DHF/DSS risk. DENV-2 is predominant in Bangladesh 2024 (70%).
📅 Disease Phases — Natural History

🌡️ Febrile Phase

Day 1–5 • High fever, myalgia, rash • Leucopenia, mild thrombocytopenia

⚠️ Critical Phase

Day 3–6 (DHF only) • Plasma leakage 24–48 hrs • Equilibrium phase may follow

✅ Convalescent Phase

2–5 days • Reabsorption of fluid • Fluid overload risk ↑

🚨 Classic DF has NO critical phase. Critical phase in DHF starts around Day 3–5 at defervescence and lasts only 24–48 hrs. Identifying its onset and end is the cornerstone of safe fluid management. An equilibrium phase may occur before convalescence.

🔍 Dengue Case Classification — WHO 2024

🟢 Without Warning Signs

Fever 2–7 days + 2 of:

  • Nausea/vomiting
  • Rash
  • Aches & pains
  • Tourniquet test (+)
  • Leucopenia WBC <5000

🟡 With Warning Signs ⚠️

  • Intense abdominal pain
  • Persistent vomiting ≥3/hr or ≥4/6hr
  • Fluid accumulation
  • Mucosal bleeding
  • Altered GCS <15
  • Hepatomegaly ≥2cm
  • Progressive ↑HCT (×2 readings, 6hr apart)

🔴 Severe Dengue

  • Severe plasma leakage → DSS or respiratory distress
  • Severe bleeding (clinical eval)
  • Severe organ involvement — AST/ALT ≥1000, impaired consciousness, organ failure
High-Risk Groups — Admit Even Without Warning Signs
  • Pregnant women • Elderly (>65 yrs) • Obesity
  • Diabetes mellitus • Hypertension, CAD, heart failure
  • Chronic renal / liver disease • Haemolytic diseases, thalassaemia
  • Immunocompromised • On steroids, NSAIDs, anticoagulants
  • Adverse social circumstances
🏠 Home Management (Outpatient)

✅ DO

  • Adequate bed rest under mosquito net
  • Oral fluids (ORS, coconut water, fruit juice, soup): 2400 ml/day (2 ml/kg/hr up to 50 kg)
  • Paracetamol only: max 3 g/day adults; 60 mg/kg/24hr children
  • Tepid sponging
  • Monitor urine every 3–4 hrs
  • Review daily with CBC from Day 3 (with SGOT/SGPT)
  • Avoid dark-coloured food/drinks (confusion with melaena)

❌ AVOID

  • Aspirin, Mefenamic acid, Ibuprofen, NSAIDs, COX-2 inhibitors
  • Steroids
  • Antibiotics (unless proven co-infection)
  • IM injections
  • Plain water only (use electrolyte fluids)
  • Warfarin/aspirin/clopidogrel — temporarily withhold
🚨 Return immediately if: Deterioration at defervescence • Unable to take orals • Severe abdominal pain • Lethargy/restlessness • Any bleeding • No urine 4–6 hrs • Cold/clammy extremities • Breathing difficulty

💧 Fluid Management — Critical Phase (Adults)
Fluid Quota = Maintenance + 5% Deficit (50 ml/kg)

Holliday-Segar Maintenance

  • 1st 10 kg → 100 ml/kg/day
  • 2nd 10 kg → 50 ml/kg/day
  • Each kg beyond 20 → 20 ml/kg/day

50 kg Adult Example

  • 1st 10 kg = 1000 ml; 2nd 10 kg = 500 ml; 20–50 kg = 600 ml → Maint = 2100 ml
  • 5% deficit = 2500 ml
  • Total = 4600 ml (48 hrs)
💡 HCT response rule: Crystalloid 500 ml → HCT ↓1–2 pts (if >3 pts, suspect bleeding). Dextran 500 ml → HCT ↓8–10 pts (if more, bleeding). Blood transfusion → HCT ↑3–5 pts/unit.
Non-Shock Critical Stage: Start at 40 ml/hr (adults). Reduce 7→5→3→1.5 ml/kg/hr as stable → stop 24–48 hrs.

Crystalloids (First Choice)

  • 0.9% NaCl (NS) — preferred
  • Hartmann's solution — preferred
  • 5% DRL / 5% DRA
  • 0.45% NS — children <6 months

Colloids (Rescue)

  • Dextran-40 (max 30 ml/kg/day)
  • 6% HES (Voluven/Plasmasol)
  • Human Albumin, Plasma, Haemaccel
  • Blood & blood components
⚠️ Colloids indicated: Shock not responding to 2 crystalloid boluses with rising HCT • Fluid quota nearly exceeded • Shock + fluid overload • Pulse pressure <10 mmHg

💊 Bleeding, Calcium, Glucose & Electrolytes

Suspect Severe Bleeding When:

  • Unstable haemodynamics regardless of HCT level
  • HCT drops after fluid bolus + unstable vitals
  • Refractory shock after 40–60 ml/kg fluid
  • Hypotension + inappropriately low/normal HCT
  • Persistent metabolic acidosis

Blood Transfusion Indicated:

  • Overt bleeding >10% or 6–8 ml/kg
  • HCT drop <40% (<45% males) after resuscitation
  • Hypotensive shock + low/normal HCT
  • Refractory shock after 40–60 ml/kg
⚠️ Platelet transfusion — very limited role. Fresh whole blood usually sufficient. Only for very severe thrombocytopenia needing urgent surgery, or clinical judgement. FFP/platelet concentrate for severe bleeding may worsen fluid overload.

Calcium

10% Ca gluconate 10 ml over 10 min; repeat 6-hrly × 24 hrs. Indications: shock not responding to 2 boluses, any shock, >2 units PRC, myocarditis.

Glucose

Both hyper- and hypoglycaemia occur. Monitor 1–2 hrly. Hypoglycaemia: 0.1–0.5 g/kg glucose IV (not in resuscitation fluid). Target BSL 80–100 mg/dl.

Electrolytes

Hyponatraemia (most common) — isotonic fluid prevents/corrects. Hypokalaemia — KCl in parenteral fluid. Metabolic acidosis — correct shock first; NaHCO₃ NOT for pH ≥7.10.

✅ Recovery Signs & Discharge Criteria

Signs of Recovery

  • Stable pulse, BP, breathing rate
  • Normal temperature
  • No external/internal bleeding
  • Return of appetite; no vomiting/abdominal pain
  • Good urinary output
  • Stable HCT at baseline
  • Convalescent rash / itching extremities

Discharge Criteria

  • No fever ≥24 hrs (without antipyretics)
  • ≥2 days after recovery from shock
  • Good appetite, improving nutrition
  • Normal HCT at baseline (or 38–40% if unknown)
  • No pleural effusion distress / no ascites
  • Platelet >50,000/mm³
  • No complications
👶 Why Children Need Special Attention
  • Higher body water content → more prone to fluid shifts during plasma leakage
  • Smaller blood volume (80 ml/kg) → minor plasma loss causes major haemodynamic instability
  • More prone to fluid overload — smaller CV and renal capacity
  • Younger children cannot express symptoms clearly → warning signs missed
  • Abdominal symptoms more common than adults
  • Co-infections common — cough/coryza does NOT exclude dengue
  • Negative tourniquet test does NOT exclude dengue
📊 In Bangladesh 2024, 19.3% of dengue cases were children aged 0–15 years (up from 17% in 2023). No age is immune.

🤱 Impact of Pregnancy on Dengue

Physiological Challenges

  • HCT rise masked by haemodilution (↑plasma vol 2nd/3rd trimester)
  • Gravid uterus — third-space fluid hard to detect
  • Baseline BP lower, pulse pressure wider, HR higher
  • Lower HCO₃ baseline
  • Serial HCT monitoring crucial

Impact on Fetus/Pregnancy

  • Early abortion (3–13%)
  • Preterm birth (3–33%)
  • Low birth weight (9–16%)
  • IUD/stillbirth (4.7–13%)
  • APH, PPH, increased C/S incidence
  • Vertical transmission 1.6–10.5%
⚠️ All pregnant women with acute fever MUST be admitted — regardless of platelet count — for close monitoring by obstetric and medical teams (MDT). Tertiary care for DSS/DHF with complications.

👶 Labour, Delivery & Postpartum Management

✅ DO

  • Hospital delivery; blood available; MDT (Obstetrician, Internist, Anaesthetist, Neonatologist)
  • Active management of 3rd stage of labour (AMTSL) + Oxytocin infusion post-delivery
  • Misoprostol/Tranexamic acid 1 g IV (repeat if bleeding >30 min) for PPH
  • Prophylactic balloon tamponade if delivery during critical phase
  • Encourage breastfeeding; early cord clamping
  • Cord blood: CBC, NS1, Dengue Ab, blood group

❌ AVOID

  • Medical termination / MR / D&C / amniocentesis in acute dengue
  • Elective C/S or induction of labour during critical phase
  • Episiotomy (unless inevitable delivery)
  • Instrumental delivery
  • IM injections / skin pricks for glucose
  • Prophylactic platelets (unless plt <50,000 & delivery in 6 hrs)
⚕️ Dengue Complications — Management Summary

Dengue Encephalopathy

  • IV fluid ≤80% maintenance; switch to colloid if large vol + leakage
  • Head up 30°; early intubation if needed
  • IV Mannitol; Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV q6–8h
  • Lactulose 5–10 ml q6h; blood glucose 80–100 mg/dl
  • Vitamin K₁ IV; anticonvulsants as indicated

Dengue Myocarditis

  • Troponin I, CK-MB, ECG, Echocardiogram
  • Myocardial dysfunction does NOT preclude fluid management
  • Empirical calcium (hypocalcaemia common in large effusions)
  • Avoid fluid overload (→ pulmonary oedema)
  • Dobutamine/milrinone for low cardiac output

HLH (Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis)

  • Suspect: fever >6 days + worsening cytopaenia + organomegaly
  • HLH-2004 criteria (≥5 of 8); ferritin ≥500 μg/L
  • Dexamethasone IV 10 mg/m²/day → oral taper
  • IV Immunoglobulin 0.75 g/kg stat + 0.4 g/kg × 3 days
  • Etoposide as rescue therapy

Fluid Overload Management

  • Stop IV fluid if not in shock
  • Furosemide IV: 1 mg/kg adults (40 mg); 0.1 mg/kg child
  • Dextran-40: 10 ml/kg bolus with furosemide midway
  • Insert urinary catheter; hourly UOP
  • No urine → check CVP/lactate → consider dialysis
  • Pleural/peritoneal tapping if severe respiratory distress

💎 PEARLs — Bedside Decision Tips

✅ DO

  • Paracetamol for fever/myalgia
  • Haemodynamic assessment before & after every fluid bolus
  • IV fluids for repeated vomiting or rapidly rising HCT
  • Appropriate isotonic IV fluids in correct time and dose
  • Tight glycaemic control
  • Colloid/PCV/FWB if indicated
  • Notify public health authorities about suspected dengue

❌ DON'T

  • Send home without follow-up instructions
  • Give NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, mefenamic acid)
  • Give IV fluids to mild dengue who can take orals
  • Give excessive fluid, blood, blood products
  • Give antibiotics for dengue alone
  • Change IV infusion rate rapidly/abruptly
  • Insert NGT to check concealed bleeding
Bedside Quick Tips
  • Leucopenia <5000/mm³ → patient will become afebrile within 24 hrs and enter critical phase
  • Hb × 3 ≈ HCT level
  • Sudden pallor = internal bleeding
  • Surface/mild bleeding → HCT higher. Deep/massive bleeding → HCT lower
  • Sudden haemodynamic deterioration refractory to fluid → suspect blood loss
  • Haemorrhage during febrile phase = DF with unusual haemorrhage, probably NOT DHF
  • Haemorrhage without fever → critically assess for DHF

🫁 Mechanical Ventilation & ICU Discharge

Indications for MV

  • ARDS — PaO₂ <60 mmHg on high-flow O₂
  • PaCO₂ >50 mmHg + respiratory acidosis
  • RR >35/min with respiratory fatigue
  • Hypotension unresponsive to fluid (lactate >4 mmol/L)
  • Encephalopathy with loss of airway reflexes
  • Massive GI bleeding / haemoptysis

Lung-Protective Strategy (ARDS)

  • Tidal volume: 4–6 ml/kg ideal body weight
  • Plateau pressure <30 cmH₂O
  • SpO₂ >92%; avoid hyperoxia
  • Adjust PEEP to oxygenation
  • Consider prone positioning in severe ARDS
ICU Discharge Criteria
  • MAP ≥65 mmHg without vasopressors; adequate CRFT; normal HR
  • HCT normalised; no effusions on exam/USG
  • No active bleeding ≥24–48 hrs; platelet ≥50,000
  • UOP >0.5 ml/kg/hr without RRT; no encephalopathy
  • Stable oxygenation on minimal/no supplemental O₂
  • Able to tolerate oral intake; no ICU-level complications

🏘️ Household Prevention Measures
  • Cover all water storage containers tightly to prevent egg-laying by Aedes mosquito
  • Empty and dry water tanks, coolers, flower pots, pet water bowls, plant drip trays at least once weekly
  • Discard waste articles (tyres etc.) that hold stagnant water
  • Check gutters and flat roofs regularly for blockage
  • Spray commercially available pyrethroid aerosols; close room 15–20 min
  • Spray timing: early morning or late afternoon (Ae. aegypti peak biting time)
  • Wear full-sleeved clothing during daytime; use commercially available repellents
  • Use insecticide-treated mosquito nets during day sleep
  • Fit doors and windows with mosquito screens/wire mesh
  • Introduce larvivorous fish (Gambusia/Guppy) in ornamental water tanks
🏫 Institutional & Community Measures
  • Designate a nodal officer for weekly larval habitat inspection at institutions
  • Ensure overhead tanks have mosquito-proof lids and screened overflow pipes
  • Organise dry day campaigns — empty all containers weekly
  • Carry out indoor space spraying with pyrethrum 2% or cyphenothrin
  • Promote personal protective measures in community
  • Report all fever cases (suspected dengue) to local health authorities
  • Apply temephos granules (1 ppm) when containers cannot be emptied
  • Mobilise households to cooperate during spraying/fogging campaigns

1
Diagnose & Grade Severity
Confirm glucose ≥200 mg/dL (or known DM) + β-OHB ≥3 mmol/L + pH <7.3 or HCO₃ <18. Grade to set care setting: Mild → ward/obs Moderate → HDU Severe → ICU. Search for a precipitant: infection, missed insulin, new-onset T1DM, MI, SGLT2 use, pancreatitis.
2
Baseline Investigations
Capillary glucose, venous blood gas, β-OHB (POC preferred), U&E, creatinine, phosphate, FBC, blood cultures if febrile. Recheck electrolytes/β-OHB/venous pH every 4 h; glucose every 1–2 h.
2024 update: arterial blood gas no longer required — venous pH is sufficient and avoids arterial puncture risk.
3
IV Fluid Resuscitation
First 2–4 h: isotonic saline or balanced crystalloid at 500–1,000 mL/h — balanced solutions now preferred (~5 h faster resolution). Switch to 0.45% NaCl once volume restored if corrected Na⁺ normal/high; continue 0.9% NaCl if low. Typical deficit ~6 L; replace 50% in first 8–12 h. Reduce bolus to 250 mL in cardiac/renal compromise or elderly.
4
Potassium — Check Before Insulin
K⁺ <3.5 hold insulin, replace ~10 mmol/h until K⁺ >3.5 • K⁺ 3.5–5.0 add 20–30 mmol/L IV fluid, start insulin • K⁺ >5.0 start insulin, withhold K⁺, recheck 2 h. Target 4.0–5.0 mmol/L throughout.
5
Insulin Therapy
Severe DKA (ICU): fixed-rate IV infusion 0.1 units/kg/h — no bolus. Mild/uncomplicated moderate DKA: SC rapid-acting analogue q1–2h now acceptable (2024). Continue home basal insulin at usual dose. Target glucose ~200 mg/dL (11 mmol/L).
When glucose <250 mg/dL: add 5–10% dextrose to IV so insulin can continue until ketonaemia resolves — do not reduce insulin just to chase glucose.
6
Bicarbonate & Phosphate — Selective Use Only
Bicarbonate: not routine — consider 100 mmol NaHCO₃ over 1 h only if pH <7.0 with haemodynamic compromise. Phosphate: not routine — replace if <1.0 mmol/L with muscle weakness, cardiac or respiratory impairment (20–30 mmol KPO₄ in IV fluid).
7
Resolution & SC Transition
Resolved when β-OHB <0.6 mmol/L + (venous pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃ ≥18) + glucose <200 mg/dL — see Diagnosis tab table. Estimate TDD 0.3–0.6 u/kg/day; start basal-bolus SC, giving basal ≥1–2 h before stopping IV infusion.
8
Discharge Planning & Prevention
Sick-day rules, insulin omission risks, ketone monitoring and injection-technique education. Ketone testing supplies at discharge. Address the precipitant. Outpatient diabetes follow-up within 1–4 weeks.
1
Recognition — Don't Be Falsely Reassured by Normal Glucose
euDKA: glucose <250 mg/dL + β-OHB ≥3 mmol/L + pH <7.3 or HCO₃ <18. Seen with SGLT2 inhibitor use, reduced carbohydrate intake/fasting/alcohol, pregnancy with T1DM, or partial insulin treatment.
🔴 Normal glucose leads to missed diagnosis. Always check β-OHB in any unwell SGLT2 user — even with normal glucose.
2
Investigations
β-OHB (POC), venous blood gas, U&E, glucose, anion gap. Urine ketones 2+ or higher support the diagnosis if β-OHB unavailable. ECG and septic screen as indicated. Recheck β-OHB, pH, electrolytes every 4 h.
3
Hold SGLT2 Inhibitor Immediately
Discontinue on admission. Do not restart until eating/drinking normally, ketonaemia resolved, and endocrinology has reviewed — withhold at least 3 days post-resolution.
4
IV Fluids
Isotonic saline or balanced crystalloid 500–1,000 mL/h for 2–4 h, then titrate. Fluid deficit often smaller than classic DKA — hydration alone reduces ketone load.
5
Potassium — Same Protocol as DKA
Check K⁺ before insulin. Replace aggressively if <3.5 mmol/L and hold insulin until corrected. Target 4.0–5.0 mmol/L.
6
Insulin + Dextrose — Key Distinction from Classic DKA
Run dextrose alongside insulin from the start: 5–10% dextrose concurrently with or before insulin; IV insulin 0.05–0.1 units/kg/h (lower end if glucose near-normal). Target glucose 140–200 mg/dL. Continue until β-OHB <0.6 mmol/L and (pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃ ≥18) — do not stop early.
The goal is to clear ketones, not to lower glucose — dextrose is what lets insulin continue safely.
7
Resolution & SC Transition
β-OHB <0.6 mmol/L + (venous pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃ ≥18) + eating and drinking normally. Transition to basal-bolus SC insulin, giving basal ≥1–2 h before stopping IV infusion.
8
Discharge — SGLT2 Counselling
Educate on euDKA risk with SGLT2 inhibitors during illness, fasting, surgery or alcohol use. Check ketones (blood preferred) whenever unwell, regardless of glucose. Joint decision on restarting SGLT2 after specialist review.
1
Diagnosis & Severity — HHS: ICU if severe/AMS
Requires all four: glucose ≥600 mg/dL (≥33.3 mmol/L), effective osmolality >300 mOsm/kg (or total >320), β-OHB <3.0 mmol/L, pH ≥7.3 and HCO₃ ≥15 mmol/L.
🔴 ICU/HDU for severe HHS, altered mental status, critical precipitating illness, or haemodynamic instability — mild/moderate HHS with normal mentation may be managed on a monitored ward bed per local protocol. Severe dehydration, hyperosmolality, high VTE risk regardless of setting. Mixed HHS/DKA (ketonaemia + hyperosmolality) → treat as DKA at 0.1 units/kg/h.
2
Investigations & Monitoring
Glucose, U&E, creatinine, serum osmolality, FBC, coagulation screen, β-OHB and venous pH (exclude mixed DKA/HHS). ECG, septic workup — infection is the commonest precipitant. Recheck glucose, creatinine, electrolytes, osmolality every 4 h.
Effective osmolality = 2×[Na⁺] + glucose (mmol/L). Exclude urea — it crosses cell membranes freely and doesn't drive tonicity.
3
IV Fluid Resuscitation — Primary Treatment
Fluid deficit often 8–12 L; fluids are the cornerstone. Start 0.9% NaCl or balanced crystalloid 500–1,000 mL/h × 2–4 h, then switch to 0.45% NaCl once volume restored, titrating by haemodynamics + osmolality. Limits: glucose fall ≤90–120 mg/dL/h, sodium fall ≤10 mmol/L/24h, osmolality fall ≤3–8 mOsm/kg/h. Use 250 mL boluses in elderly/cardiac/renal impairment.
Fluids alone often lower glucose substantially — insulin is adjunctive here, not the primary therapy.
4
Insulin — Low Dose, Start After Fluids
Begin only after initial fluid resuscitation. Fixed-rate IV infusion 0.05 units/kg/h (lower than DKA to avoid rapid osmolality shifts); mixed HHS/DKA → 0.1 units/kg/h. Add 5–10% dextrose when glucose <250 mg/dL to permit continued insulin.
5
Thromboprophylaxis
High VTE risk from extreme dehydration and hyperviscosity. Start LMWH (e.g. enoxaparin) unless contraindicated; continue until fully mobile. Consider therapeutic anticoagulation in very high-risk cases (immobile, prior VTE).
6
Resolution Criteria
Osmolality <300 mOsm/kg + glucose <250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) + alert, euvolaemic, tolerating oral intake. Transition to SC basal-bolus insulin, basal ≥1–2 h before stopping infusion.
7
Discharge & Prevention
Identify and treat precipitant (infection most common; also stroke, MI, medications). Structured education emphasising hydration and sick-day rules. Review hyperglycaemia-worsening drugs (steroids, antipsychotics, thiazides). Follow-up within 1–4 weeks; consider CGM to detect early deterioration.

BLS & ACLS Algorithm (AHA 2025) ▼
BLS — SEQUENCE
1. Check response → Shout for help → Get AED
2. Check pulse ≤10 sec → If absent or unsure: start CPR
3. Compressions: 100–120/min | Depth 5–6cm
   Hard surface | Allow full recoil | Minimize interruptions
4. Ratio: 30:2  |  Untrained → Hands-only CPR
5. AED: Attach → Analyse → Shock if advised → Resume CPR immediately
6. Advanced airway placed: 10 breaths/min async
2025: CPR feedback devices recommended • Passive O₂ during compressions acceptable
ACLS — SHOCKABLE (VF / Pulseless VT)
Shock (120–200J) → 2min CPR → Check rhythm → Shock
3rd cycle: Adrenaline 1mg IV → repeat every 3–5 min
3rd cycle: Amiodarone 300mg IV bolus
5th cycle: Amiodarone 150mg IV (one further dose)
  OR Lidocaine 1–1.5mg/kg if Amiodarone unavailable
ACLS — NON-SHOCKABLE (PEA / Asystole)
CPR → Adrenaline 1mg IV ASAP → repeat every 3–5 min
Search & treat reversible causes:
5H: Hypoxia • Hypovolaemia • H⁺ (acidosis) • Hypo/Hyperkalaemia • Hypothermia
5T: Tension PTX • Tamponade • Toxins • Thrombosis PE • Thrombosis MI

12-lead ECG → PCI if STEMI
